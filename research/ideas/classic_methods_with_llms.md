# Classic creativity and product-thinking methods with LLMs: evidence for humans, evidence with models

Research date: 2026-10-26. Notes are in English.

- Items marked **[search]** come from search-result snippets and summaries. The full text was not opened.

## Q1. For each method: what is the empirical evidence in human creativity research? Which methods are folklore and which are supported?

### Takeaway
Only three ideas have solid human evidence:
- constraints help in moderation (an inverted U);
- near or moderately distant analogies beat random "far" ones for useful ideas;
- creativity training in general works, while unstructured group brainstorming underperforms individuals working alone.

The branded methods have little rigorous evidence. Design thinking, TRIZ, Six Thinking Hats and SCAMPER rest mostly on case studies, small classroom quasi-experiments or literature reviews without causal tests. De Bono's lateral thinking and Po, Oblique Strategies, "How Might We" and morphological analysis are essentially folklore or practitioner craft, with no controlled evidence found.

### Cited findings

**Design thinking (empathize-first)**
- A review of 164 articles (Rösch, Tiberius & Kraus, *European Journal of Innovation Management*, 2023) builds a framework of context factors, process and outcomes. It is a holistic overview, not a causal test. [search] — [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/EJIM-03-2022-0164/full/html)
- A 2014–2024 systematic review of 57 articles on product innovation frames design thinking as a "dynamic capability". It reports obstacles: cultural resistance, difficulty measuring results, and friction with established innovation models. Its framework is not empirically tested. [search] — [Turku thesis repository](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/25777)
- A health-care systematic review of 24 studies found 12 successful, 11 with mixed success and 1 unsuccessful. Only 4 compared design thinking with traditional approaches, and those 4 favoured design thinking on satisfaction, usability and effectiveness. The authors flag methodological and quality limitations. [search] — [CDC, *Preventing Chronic Disease* 2018](https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2018/18_0128.htm) / [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6178900/)
- A teamwork review (Schlott, 2024) found effects on interactions, mindset and performance, but the effects vary. [search] — [Journal of Organization Design via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jorgde/v13y2024i4d10.1007_s41469-024-00177-x.html)

**Jobs To Be Done**
- No peer-reviewed controlled evaluation of JTBD as an ideation method was found. The practitioner core, Moesta's switch interview, holds that hypothetical feedback is useless and anchors on a real recent purchase, with four forces (push, pull, anxiety, habit). The search result attributes this to Moesta. [search] — [Intercom interview with Bob Moesta](https://www.intercom.com/blog/interview-bob-moesta-part-2-of-2/); [Koji summary of the switch-interview method](https://www.koji.so/docs/switch-interviews-jtbd-method) (vendor)

**TRIZ**
- A controlled experiment comparing TRIZ with control groups ran at three universities (UTEP, Penn State, Maryland): graduate and undergraduate students, two problems, collaborative and individual use (Vargas Hernandez, ASEE 2012). The results were not visible in the snippets. [search] — [ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/experimental-assessment-of-triz-effectiveness-in-idea-generation)
- A Politecnico di Milano thesis compared brainstorming, a problem–solution matrix, a technical-contradiction map (TRIZ-derived) and patent far-field text in an SME setting. The contradiction map gave the highest novelty, quantity and variety, but on one sample system. [search] — [POLITesi](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/handle/10589/132061)
- Livotov (ICED 2023) notes that "few authors have focused on the comparative analysis of structured ideation" outcomes, which signals a fragmented evidence base. [search] — [Design Society](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/46373/enhancing_engineering_creativity_with_automated_formulation_of_elementary_solution_principles)
- The TRIZ literature review by Ilevbare, Probert & Phaal (*Technovation*, 2013) surveyed practitioners. It is mainly survey and case evidence and points to complexity and a steep learning curve as barriers. [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1016/j.technovation.2012.11.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.technovation.2012.11.003)
- The AutoTRIZ authors themselves say TRIZ's "complexity and reliance on users' knowledge, experience, and reasoning capabilities limit its practicality". [fetched] — [arXiv 2403.13002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13002)

**SCAMPER**
- No controlled human study of SCAMPER was retrieved in this session (see Gaps).

**De Bono: lateral thinking, Po, CoRT, Six Thinking Hats**
- **Göçmen & Coşkun (2019)** ran a two-experiment brainstorming study. The yellow hat beat the red hat on unique and creative ideas. In the second experiment the green hat produced more unique ideas than yellow or red. This tests single hats as instructions, not the full method. [search] — [Bolu Abant İzzet Baysal University repository](https://acikerisim.ibu.edu.tr/items/467189ce-cbe8-4c0f-8ad5-abe87827fb97)
- **School studies** are small quasi-experiments that report gains: 80 tenth-graders in Tulkarm, 40 kindergarteners in Thailand, and 15 experimental against 36 control students. [search] — [JPERC, Baghdad](https://jperc.uobaghdad.edu.iq/index.php/jperc/article/view/458); [Chulalongkorn](https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/handle/123456789/11067)
- **A single-case study** of 4 honours students at UNC Charlotte found a functional relation for using the hats as perspectives. It found none for the number of creative ideas. [search] — [UNC Charlotte graduate school](https://graduateschool.charlotte.edu/?p=4595)
- **A review of 42 thinking frameworks**, reached through a Wikipedia-derived page and not verified at its original source, concluded there is "sparse research evidence" that general thinking gains can be attributed to CoRT or Thinking Hats. [search] — [Wikipedia: Edward de Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_de_Bono)
- **Critics** call lateral thinking pseudo-scientific and say it has never been rigorously tested. No controlled trials of Po or provocation were found. [search] — [Wikipedia: Edward de Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_de_Bono); [Murdoch: evaluation of de Bono's CoRT](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-evaluation-of-the-De-Bono/991005541951807891)

**Brian Eno's Oblique Strategies**
- The only empirical item found is a small Leiden University pilot: an Alternative Uses Task with three Oblique Strategies prompts against no prompt, plus transformer-generated cards. Its results were not confirmed. [search] — [Leiden thesis, Mkrtchyan 2021–22](https://theses.liacs.nl/pdf/2021-2022-MkrtchyanW.pdf)

**Morphological analysis**
- The pre-LLM computerised morphological-analysis literature reports reducing millions of configurations by more than 95% to internally consistent ones. That is a claim about search efficiency, not a creativity outcome. [search] — [Garvey et al., Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3021720/)

**How Might We**
- No empirical study of the "How Might We" framing was found (see Gaps).

**Analogical and far-transfer reasoning**
- **Chan, Dow & Schunn (*Design Studies* 36, 2015; Best Paper of 2015).** They studied hundreds of concepts across a dozen challenges on an open innovation platform. Citing sources was linked to more creative ideas, but *closer* sources helped more than distant ones. The inverse relationship held across problems, and the paper calls the far-source hypothesis "popular but unevenly supported". [search] — [Pitt LRDC preprint](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chan-Dow-Schun-desstudies-nearfar-inpress.pdf); [CMU HCII news, "Think outside the box? Not so fast"](https://www.hcii.cmu.edu/news/think-outside-box-not-so-fast)
- **A DRS 2020 study** found distant sources raise novelty and originality, while near sources support quality and detail. [search] — [DRS 2020](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2020/researchpapers/99)
- **Fu et al. (2013)** in the *Journal of Mechanical Design* found that analogies of "medium" distance helped most and that very far ones hurt. [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1115/1.4023158](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4023158)
- **Kittur and Shahaf's lab** (Gilon et al., CHI 2018, "Analogy mining for specific design needs") retrieved analogies matched on purpose but different in mechanism. In a user study these raised the likelihood of creative ideas. That detail comes through a third-party summary. [search] — [ar5iv 1712.06880](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1712.06880); see also Hope, Chan, Kittur & Shahaf, KDD 2017, "Accelerating innovation through analogy mining" [prior knowledge, arXiv link] — [arXiv 1706.05585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05585)

**Constraint-based creativity**
- Acar, Tarakci & van Knippenberg (*Journal of Management* 45(1), 2019) is a cross-disciplinary integrative review. Secondary sources summarise it as an inverted U: too few constraints leave too many equally plausible directions, and too many stifle. The field is called fragmented and conflicting. Later work offers an interaction view (the IConIC model) as an alternative. [search] — [City, University of London accepted manuscript](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/20459/)

**Background facts relevant to all methods**
- Interacting brainstorming groups produce fewer ideas than the same number of people working alone (nominal groups), largely because of production blocking (Diehl & Stroebe 1987). [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.53.3.497](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.53.3.497)
- Design fixation: showing an example makes designers copy its features, including its flaws (Jansson & Smith 1991). [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1016/0142-694X(91)90003-F](https://doi.org/10.1016/0142-694X(91)90003-F)
- A meta-analysis of creativity training found it effective overall, with larger effects for cognitively grounded programmes such as problem identification and conceptual combination (Scott, Leritz & Mumford 2004). [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1080/10400410409534549](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400410409534549)

### Inferences
- **Supported:** moderate constraints; near-to-medium analogies, especially purpose-matched with a different mechanism; structured creativity training in general; working alone before working together.
- **Partially supported:** design thinking (outcome association, weak causality); TRIZ (small engineering studies, contradiction-map variants); single-hat instructions such as "green hat = generate novel ideas".
- **Folklore or craft with no controlled evidence found:** Po and provocation, Oblique Strategies, HMW, SCAMPER as such, morphological analysis as a creativity booster, JTBD as an ideation method. JTBD is a research and interviewing discipline whose value claims rest on cases.
- The strongest *mechanism-level* human finding is that "far" is not automatically better. That directly contradicts a common assumption in LLM creativity prompting ("make wild, distant analogies").

### Gaps
- No controlled human SCAMPER study was retrieved this session. Education studies are believed to exist but were not verified.
- No empirical study of "How Might We" phrasing was found.
- No controlled study of Oblique Strategies beyond a small pilot whose results were not confirmed.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of JTBD's effect on idea quality.
- The ASEE 2012 TRIZ experiment's results could not be read.

## Q2. Papers and systems that use these methods with LLMs: what were the measured results against plain prompting?

### Takeaway
The best direct test, IDEAFix (2026: 5 LLMs, 25 strategies), found three things:
- every method-inspired prompt beats a bare control;
- *complex method structure (C-K, SCAMPER) does not reliably raise novelty*, and simple explicit divergence cues ("think outside the box", "unconventional") plus "category negation" do as well or better;
- outputs from all models stay homogenized in a shared, tech-heavy "hivemind" space.

TRIZ-LLM systems (AutoTRIZ, TRIZ-GPT) are validated against textbook cases and case studies. They are not compared with plain prompting. Analogy-retrieval systems, of which BioSpark is the best evidence, do beat a control condition with human users. Chain-of-thought improves idea diversity. Execution studies show that LLM "novelty" shrinks once ideas are carried out.

### Cited findings

**Method-as-prompt benchmarks**
- **IDEAFix** (Carichon, Sharma, Girard, Rampa, Farnadi; arXiv 30 May 2026; ICML 2026 workshop) [fetched]:
  - Models tested: Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen3-30B, Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4o and Grok-4.1-fast.
  - Strategies (25): 2 control, 3 brainstorming, 4 C-K, 4 TRIZ, 4 design thinking, 3 prospective scenarios, 1 SCAMPER, and 4 "AI-specific" category-negation prompts. Category negation means generate ideas, cluster them, then generate outside those clusters.
  - Metrics: fluency, diversity, novelty, rarity, overlap and perplexity.
  - Results: all method-inspired prompts significantly outperformed control. Two category-negation prompts and one brainstorming prompt were significantly higher on novelty, with TRIZ close. C-K and SCAMPER "did not reliably improve novelty". No single method maximized all metrics. The authors hypothesize that explicit divergence cues drive gains more than method structure. Cross-model outputs converge strongly, with an "LLM hivemind" oriented to digital and tech solutions. Defixing attributes, meaning surprising or negative constraints, only partially mitigated this.
  - Example model averages: Llama-70B novelty 0.556, diversity 0.485; GPT-4o novelty 0.428.
  - Sources: [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00875); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00875); [ICML slides](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2026/Slides/68567.pdf)
- **Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch**, "Prompting Diverse Ideas" (arXiv, Jan 2024). They used GPT-4 on consumer product ideas for college students under $50. GPT-4 idea pools with plausible prompts were less diverse than those of human groups. Prompt engineering substantially improved diversity, and chain-of-thought gave the highest diversity and unique-idea count, approaching human-group level. [fetched] — [arXiv 2402.01727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01727)
- A creativity-centred survey cites Zhao et al. (2025): instructional prompting raised novelty from 3.78 to 4.05 and value from 4.46 to 4.65, scored by an LLM judge. Persona prompting scored higher than generic roles. This is secondhand. [search] — [arXiv 2511.07448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07448)

**TRIZ with LLMs**
- **AutoTRIZ** (Jiang, Li, Qian, Zhang & Luo; arXiv Mar 2024, revised Mar 2025; *Advanced Engineering Informatics* 65, 2025, 103312). It automates the TRIZ steps: problem, contradiction detection, principles from a knowledge base, and a solution report. Evaluation consists of consistency and comparison against textbook cases plus one real application, battery thermal management. The abstract mentions no comparison with plain LLM prompting or human experts. The authors propose extending the approach to SCAMPER, Design Heuristics and Design-by-Analogy. [fetched] — [arXiv 2403.13002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13002)
- **TRIZ-GPT** (Chen, Song, Ding, Sun, Childs & Zuo; arXiv 12 Aug 2024). It uses TRIZ case collections and stepwise reasoning prompts. In a mechanical-engineering case study, GPT-4 solutions "closely resonate with original solutions". The results are qualitative, with no baseline comparison. [fetched] — [arXiv 2408.05897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05897)

**Analogy systems with LLMs**
- **BioSpark** (Kang, Kittur et al., CMU; arXiv Mar 2025). An LLM finds, maps and adapts biological analogies. It was evaluated through designer workshops plus a controlled user study. Against a control condition it produced more ideas, ideas rated higher in creative quality, and more diverse biological inspirations used. [search] — [arXiv 2503.09838](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09838v1); precursor [arXiv 2312.11388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11388) (NeurIPS 2023 workshop, case studies)
- **Kokate & Onkar** (*Design Science*, Cambridge, 2026). LLM-generated analogical stimuli were tested with 24 undergraduate design students, and the tool-assisted groups produced higher-rated ideas. The sample is small. [search] — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/0B8149CCF53C45E1E1B78C4CA93E5BDC)
- "Assessing the Effect of Cross-Domain Mapping on Creativity in Humans and LLMs" (arXiv 2603.19087, 2026) exists, but its findings were not retrieved. — [arXiv 2603.19087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19087)

**Six Thinking Hats and role-play**
- **PTFA** (arXiv 2503.12499, 2025): each LLM agent embodies one hat to facilitate live human consensus discussions. Its framing is that it reduces cognitive burden, not that it raises idea quality. [search] — [arXiv 2503.12499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12499)
- **"LLM Discussion"** (arXiv 2405.06373, 2024) uses a discussion framework with role-play to raise LLM creativity. Its appendix includes a Six Hats variant, whose main results were not retrieved. [search] — [arXiv 2405.06373](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06373)
- **Multi-agent research ideation** (SIGDIAL 2025) does not use the hats. Multiple agents with complementary expertise and multi-turn refinement improved quality in diversity metrics and a GPT-4 preference tournament. [search] — [arXiv 2507.08350](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08350v1)

**Morphological analysis and combination**
- A DRS 2024 paper proposes LLM-augmented morphological analysis for conceptual design. Only the abstract was seen, so results are unknown. [search] — [DRS 2024](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2024/researchpapers/152)
- A combinatorial prompting paper (arXiv 2508.19200) mines elements from papers and prompts curated combinations. It reports diverse, relevant and grounded research ideas. [search] — [arXiv 2508.19200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19200v3)

**Oblique Strategies**
- Joel Simon's "Lluminate" has reasoning LLMs follow creative-strategy procedures, including Oblique Strategies. It is a practitioner experiment, not a controlled study. [search] — [joelsimon.net](https://www.joelsimon.net/lluminate)

**Broader LLM ideation results that bound all methods**
- **Si, Yang & Hashimoto (2024)** compared expert NLP researchers with an LLM agent. LLM ideas were judged more novel; one blog reports 5.64 against 4.84 out of 10, a figure not verified in the paper. [search] — [Can LLMs generate novel research ideas, arXiv 2409.04109 (prior knowledge link)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109)
- **Si, Hashimoto & Yang (2025)**, "The Ideation-Execution Gap". Forty-three experts executed ideas for more than 100 hours each. After execution, LLM ideas' scores dropped more than human ones on novelty, excitement, effectiveness and overall rating, and rankings flipped on many metrics. [search] — [arXiv 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803)
- **Doshi & Hauser** (*Science Advances* 10(28), July 2024). The study had 293 writers and 3,519 evaluations from 600 evaluators. LLM story ideas made stories more creative, better written and more enjoyable, most of all for less creative writers. The AI-assisted stories were also more similar to each other: "individually better off, collectively narrower". [search] — [PMC11244532](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)
- **Anderson, Shah & Kreminski** (C&C 2024): ChatGPT-assisted ideation was more homogeneous across users than ideation with an alternative support tool. [prior knowledge, arXiv link] — [arXiv 2402.01536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)
- **Wadinambiarachchi et al.** (CHI 2024): designers exposed to AI-generated images showed *more* fixation. [prior knowledge, arXiv link] — [arXiv 2403.11164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164)
- A review of 61 LLM-ideation studies finds LLMs used mostly for generation and refinement, rarely for scope specification, structuring foundational material or multi-idea selection. [search] — [arXiv 2503.00946](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00946v3)
- An industry comparison from Kookmin University pitted a traditional team against a GenAI team over 4 hours. The AI team produced more ideas of comparable quality, but "lack of context consistency" and "structural completeness" made expert convergence essential. [search] — [Kookmin University](https://english.kookmin.ac.kr/kookmin/people/334?pn=10)

### Inferences
- With LLMs, a method's *name and structure* matter less than (a) explicit divergence instructions, (b) procedural moves that force the model out of its first cluster (category negation, chain-of-thought, generate-then-exclude), and (c) external material injected into context (analogy retrieval, mined elements).
- Methods that work by bringing in **outside information** (BioSpark, analogy mining, combinatorial mining) have the best controlled evidence. Methods that only restructure the model's own reasoning (SCAMPER, C-K, Hats) show weak or no gains over simple cues. This is consistent with homogenization: the model's prior is the bottleneck, and restructuring a prompt does not change the prior.
- TRIZ-LLM papers show *feasibility* (the model can run the steps), not *superiority*. No head-to-head against "just ask GPT" was found.
- Human far-analogy evidence (closer is better for useful ideas) suggests "make a wild analogy" prompts may raise judged novelty but lower usefulness. This mirrors the ideation-execution gap.
- LLM-judge novelty scores, the metric used in IDEAFix and Zhao et al., may overstate value. Si et al. show novelty shrinks under execution.

### Gaps
- No study compares the full SCAMPER, Six Hats, Po, HMW or morphological-analysis procedures against plain prompting with human-rated outcomes.
- BioSpark effect sizes were not retrieved.
- The "LLM Discussion" Six Hats results were not retrieved.
- Whether Meincke et al. tested persona and design-thinking prompts (the abstract highlights only CoT) was not confirmed from the page.

## Q3. Grounding ideas in real user evidence vs imagined users: what is known about LLM-simulated users and personas?

### Takeaway
The evidence is consistent and negative for replacement and positive only for preparation.

Synthetic users are:
- over-agreeable (they praise every concept);
- idealized (they report completing what real people abandon);
- undifferentiated ("care about everything");
- stereotyped toward the "typical" profile answer, with the highest-fidelity models producing the most stereotyped populations;
- missing reluctance and walk-away behaviour.

They cannot recount real past behaviour, which is exactly what JTBD switch interviews rely on. Real-user evidence is therefore the corrective input that empathize-first and JTBD methods require. Simulated empathy reproduces the model's prior.

### Cited findings
- **NN/g, Rosala & Moran**, "Synthetic Users: If, When, and How to Use AI-Generated 'Research'" (21 June 2024). They reran three real studies with the Synthetic Users product and ChatGPT. [fetched] — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/)
  - Synthetic learners claimed they finished all their courses; the real participant had finished 3 of 7.
  - Synthetic users praised discussion forums that real learners avoided as contrived.
  - They listed 7 engagement factors with no priority: "Synthetic users seem to care about everything."
  - Concept testing (drone delivery) drew vague positivity.
  - Recommended uses: desk research, piloting interview guides, hypotheses and proto-personas. "Treat the data… as hypotheses that need testing."
  - Avoid them for niche populations.
- Secondary coverage of NN/g's survey re-run: humans produced more than 50 distinct labels against about a dozen from synthetic respondents. This is secondhand. [search] — [ZURB Radar](https://radar.zurb.com/article/nielsen-norman-synthetic-users-were-too-shallow-to-be-useful-against-three-real-studies)
- **"When Synthetic Users Fail"** (arXiv 2607.26348, 2026) is a cross-domain benchmark. Demographic profiles map to stereotyped "typical" answers. Its two headline failures, no individual-level advantage over a demographic baseline and positive stereotyping, hold across US attitudes and 63-country values for all 4 models. Aggregate-distribution success can hide individual or subgroup invalidity. [search] — [arXiv 2607.26348](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.26348)
- **"The Chameleon's Limit"** (arXiv 2604.24698, 2026): persona collapse across 10 LLMs. Variation tracks coarse demographic stereotypes, not specified individual differences, and the models with the highest per-persona fidelity produce the *most* stereotyped populations. [search] — [arXiv 2604.24698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24698)
- **"Simulated Customers Never Walk Away"** (arXiv 2606.20708, 2026) measured simulators against real purchase outcomes. Simulators inherit LLM over-agreeableness and fail to represent declining willingness in people whose willingness actually declines. [search] — [arXiv 2606.20708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20708)
- **Wang, Morgenstern & Dickerson** (*Nature Machine Intelligence* 7(3):400–411, 2025; first preprint Feb 2024). The preprint title claimed LLMs "cannot replace human participants"; the published one says they "can harmfully misportray and flatten identity groups". Two training-inherent limitations are named. Inference-time techniques reduce the harms but do not remove them, and supplementing (pilots) is acceptable while replacing is not. [search] — [arXiv 2402.01908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01908v1)
- **CoMPosT** (EMNLP 2023): GPT-4 simulations of some political and marginalized groups were highly susceptible to caricature. [search] — [arXiv 2310.11501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11501v1)
- **Pretrained persona mixture models** (arXiv 2609.22607, 2026) list bias, reduced diversity, sycophancy, over-personalization and multi-turn persona drift in instruction-tuned simulators. Base models keep more behavioural fidelity. [search] — [arXiv 2609.22607](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22607)
- **Santurkar et al.** (ICML 2023), "Whose opinions do language models reflect?": LM opinions misalign with many US demographic groups, and RLHF shifts them further. [prior knowledge, arXiv link] — [arXiv 2303.17548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17548)
- **Hämäläinen, Tavast & Kunnari** (CHI 2023): GPT-3 synthetic HCI interview answers were often rated *more* human-like than real ones. That makes them plausible-sounding, which is itself a risk; the authors frame the use as early ideation and piloting only. [prior knowledge, DOI link] — [doi:10.1145/3544548.3580688](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580688)
- **An industry position on the "bad days" framing:** research should focus on atypical, bad-day behaviour, because AI gives you the "average user". This is opinion. [search] — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/let-ai-have-the-average-user-why-user-research-should-focus-on-bad-days-e7bba55f855f)

### Inferences
- The failure modes (agreeableness, idealized behaviour, central-tendency answers) are exactly the inverse of what generative product methods need. JTBD seeks anxieties, habits and non-consumption. Empathize-first seeks surprises and workarounds. A synthetic user structurally underproduces both.
- Practical rule supported by the sources:
  - real complaints, reviews and interviews go *into* the prompt as grounding;
  - synthetic personas are used only to pilot questions or generate hypotheses;
  - anything a persona "says" is labelled a hypothesis.
- Persona prompting can raise *diversity of the model's ideas* (Zhao et al., secondhand) while still being invalid as *evidence about users*. Those are two different uses and should not be conflated.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found comparing ideas generated from real-user corpora (reviews, interview transcripts) against ideas generated from imagined personas, measured on downstream validity. This is the key missing experiment.
- No study was found of AI-moderated against human-moderated JTBD switch interviews. Vendors claim scale (Perspective AI: more than 200 parallel interviews), but give no evidence. — [Perspective AI blog](https://getperspective.ai/blog/jobs-to-be-done-interviews-the-ai-first-approach-to-running-jtbd-research-at-scale/markdown) (vendor)

## Q4. Practitioner reports (IDEO, PMs, designers) on using these methods with AI: what worked, and what produced generic output?

### Takeaway
Practitioner material is mostly framing and courses, not reported experiments. IDEO positions AI as amplifying exploration across design-thinking phases without published outcome data. NN/g is the one practitioner source with a documented comparison, and it is negative for synthetic research. Practitioner advice converges on three uses:
- method prompts (SCAMPER) for controlled variations of an existing idea;
- AI as "muse, not oracle";
- humans for convergence, context and real-user evidence.

### Cited findings
- **IDEO U** "AI × design thinking" course: IDEO designers share AI use across phases and draw on IDEO experiments, but report no results. The framing is that AI should "enhance creativity rather than replace". [search] — [IDEO U course page](https://ideou.com/products/ai-course)

### Inferences
- "Generic output" in practitioner accounts lines up with the research findings on homogenization (Doshi & Hauser, IDEAFix, Anderson et al.). The fix practitioners describe, bringing context, real data and human convergence, matches what the controlled studies support: external grounding plus anti-first-cluster procedures.
- No practitioner source was found that shows a branded method making LLM output *less generic* in a measured way.
